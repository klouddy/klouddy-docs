## Coach Discussion Prompts

### Goal

Students should leave kickoff with:

- a clear understanding of scoring
- a basic strategy choice
- a first-pass robot requirements list
- a short list of priorities and risks

### Part 1: Understand the Game

Ask:

- What scores points?
- When can each scoring action happen?
- Which scoring actions are high value?
- Which scoring actions look repeatable?
- Which ones are likely traps because they are flashy but hard?

Push students to separate:

- high points
- high difficulty
- high reliability

### Part 2: Understand Constraints

Ask:

- What limits robot design?
- What starting constraints matter?
- What interaction rules matter?
- What actions could accidentally become penalties?

Coach prompts:

- Show me the exact rule.
- Is that allowed, or are we assuming?
- Would that pass inspection?

### Part 3: Robot Restrictions and Constraints

Ask:

- What are the maximum starting size limits?
- When can the robot expand, and by how much?
- Are there height, extension, or volume limits?
- How many game pieces can the robot legally control?
- What field contact is illegal?
- What robot-to-robot contact is illegal?
- What defense is legal, and what crosses the line into penalties?
- Which mechanism ideas should we reject because they are not legal?

Coach prompts:

- Point to the exact restriction in the manual.
- Is this a hard rule or a strategy preference?
- Would this design still pass inspection after we add all mechanisms?
- What is the easiest way this robot could get a penalty?

### Part 4: Strategy

Ask:

- If we were playing our first event tomorrow, how would we try to win?
- What scores well without requiring a perfect robot?
- What part of the match matters most: auto, teleop, or endgame?
- What can be done many times per match?
- What takes too long to be worth it?

Coach prompts:

- Would you rather do a hard task once or an easy task six times?
- What wins more matches: peak score or reliable score?

### Part 5: Robot Requirements

Ask:

- What must the robot physically do?
- What game piece locations matter?
- What accuracy is needed?
- What speed is needed?
- What is the minimum robot that still helps an alliance?

Coach prompt:

- Turn strategy into requirements.
- Example: "We want to score quickly" becomes "robot must intake from floor in under X seconds."

### Part 6: Prioritize

Have students sort ideas into:

- must have
- nice to have
- stretch goals

Coach prompts:

- If build season ended in 2 weeks, what would we keep?
- What helps us score earliest and most reliably?

### Expected Outputs

Students should produce:

- a list of all scoring actions
- a ranking of scoring options by value, difficulty, and reliability
- a list of robot restrictions and penalty risks
- a chosen strategy focus
- a must-have robot feature list
- a version 1 robot scope
- a risk list

## Suggested Kickoff Outcome

By the end of kickoff, the team should be able to say:

- how they plan to score points
- which game tasks are realistic for their current team
- which robot ideas are legal and which should be avoided
- what the version 1 robot must do
- which ideas are being saved for later

# Compliant or Flap Wheel Intake

Use this page as a deeper follow-up to the quick reference, not as a full build guide.

Still deciding? Start with the [Intake Quick Reference](../intake-quick-reference.md).

## Start Here

- Pick this style when you want a forgiving intake for larger pieces with flat faces.
- Its biggest strength is controlled contact across different piece angles.
- Its biggest tradeoff is that it usually wants one piece at a time and can struggle with balls or very small pieces.
- Rough build difficulty: `medium`.
- Rough maintenance difficulty: `low to medium`.

## Why Teams Pick This Intake

GM0 recommends compliant-wheel style intakes over solid-wheel versions because they are more forgiving about piece orientation and intake angle. That makes them a strong choice when the driver will not hit every pickup perfectly.

This style is usually strongest when the gamepiece is large enough to stay trapped between wheels and guides, and when your robot benefits from a clean, repeatable handoff into the next mechanism.

`Flap wheel` is a useful team term, but most source material groups these ideas under compliant wheels, flexible flaps, or similar intake contact methods.

## What a Good Version Looks Like

- The first contact pulls the piece in instead of bouncing it away.
- The intake keeps wheel contact long enough to control the piece, not just touch it once.
- Side guides or funnels keep angled pieces from entering sideways and jamming.
- Compression is enough to grip the piece without crushing, stalling, or throwing it.
- The path from intake to the next stage stays short and visible.

Good public examples:

- [`9971 LanBros` Relic Recovery intake](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/active-intake/types-of-intakes.html#id5)
- [`8680 Kraken-Pinion` Relic Recovery intake](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/active-intake/types-of-intakes.html#id6)
- [`2856 Tesseract` Relic Recovery intake](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/active-intake/types-of-intakes.html#id7)
- [`11115 Gluten Free` Relic Recovery intake](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/active-intake/types-of-intakes.html#id8)

These examples are older FTC games, but they still show the core idea well: keep reliable wheel contact on a large, flat gamepiece.

## What to Watch Out For

- Too little compression leads to slips and missed pickups.
- Too much compression can stall motors, pinch pieces, or make jams harder to clear.
- A bad guide shape can let pieces enter at a weird angle and wedge sideways.
- If the intake works on a straight pickup but fails while turning or hitting walls, the full path is not controlled well enough yet.
- Soft wheels and flaps wear out. Fast replacement matters.

## Build Difficulty

Overall build complexity is usually `medium`.

- Easier than multi-stage conveyors.
- Harder than a simple claw or scoop.
- Usually reasonable for teams that can prototype compression and guide spacing early.

This is often a good starting active intake because it can be effective without a very deep internal transfer path.

## Maintenance and Reliability

Overall maintenance complexity is usually `low to medium`.

- Wheels, hubs, and mounting hardware should be easy to access.
- Check for bent supports after wall hits or robot contact.
- Watch for worn wheels, loose shafts, and changing compression.
- If the intake is spring-loaded, inspect the pivot and spring return often.

## Good Starting Point

- Start with a simple two-sided wheel path and short transfer.
- Use compliant wheels or other soft intake wheels intended for intake use.
- Build a quick prototype and test different compression and guide spacing with the real gamepiece.
- If the gamepiece is large and flat-sided, this is often one of the first active intake styles worth trying.

## Compare It to Other Intake Options

- Compared with a [Horizontal Roller Intake](horizontal-roller-intake.md), this style usually gives more deliberate one-piece control.
- Compared with a [Tubing or Flexible-Wheel Intake](tubing-or-flexible-wheel-intake.md), it usually trades raw pickup speed for better control.
- For a broader comparison, go back to the [Common Active Intake Styles](../intake-quick-reference.md#common-active-intake-styles) section.

## What Still Needs Confirmation

- Always confirm the current season rules, legal parts, and robot size limits.
- Re-check whether the current gamepiece shape actually rewards this style more than a simpler passive intake or a wider roller intake.

# Passive Intake or Claw

Use this page as a deeper follow-up to the quick reference, not as a full build guide.

Still deciding? Start with the [Intake Quick Reference](../intake-quick-reference.md).

## Start Here

- Pick this style when the gamepiece is awkward, large, fragile, or easier to place directly than to pull through rollers.
- Its biggest strength is simple direct control of one piece.
- Its biggest tradeoff is slower pickup and a bigger demand on driver alignment.
- Rough build difficulty: `low`.
- Rough maintenance difficulty: `low to medium`.

## Why Teams Pick This Intake

GM0 describes passive intakes as mechanisms where the driver picks up individual items, usually with a gripper. Claws are the most common passive intake style in FTC.

Teams usually pick this style when they need direct one-piece control, when the object shape is hard to manage with an active intake, or when they need a simpler mechanism they can finish early and trust.

This is also a strong option when the same mechanism can both collect and score, because that removes the need for a separate transfer path.

## What a Good Version Looks Like

- The driver can line it up quickly without fighting awkward approach angles.
- The claw, scoop, or gripper holds the piece securely without crushing it.
- The mechanism opens and closes reliably every time.
- The piece stays controlled while the robot turns, accelerates, or stops.
- The collection and scoring motions are simple enough for students to repeat under match pressure.

Good public examples:

- [RoboFTC Claw Design ](https://roboftc.github.io/cad/claw.html)
- [`11115 Gluten Free` relic claw](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/passive-intake-claw.html#id1)
- [`8680 Kraken-Pinion` relic claw](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/passive-intake-claw.html#id2)

GM0 also shows [`13075 Coram Deo Academy Robotics`](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/passive-intake-claw.html#id3) as a reminder that a claw can be a weaker fit when the game really rewards faster intake cycles.

## What to Watch Out For

- Passive pickup is usually slower because the driver must aim at one object and close the mechanism deliberately.
- Claws can be more fragile than they first appear, especially if they take side hits.
- A passive intake can become frustrating if it needs perfect alignment every time.
- If the game rewards repeated floor cycles, this style can lose too much time.
- If a claw grabs well but scores awkwardly, the overall mechanism may still be the wrong choice.

## Build Difficulty

Overall build complexity is usually `low`.

- Many passive intakes can be built with servos and simple brackets instead of dedicated motors.
- They usually have fewer moving parts than an active intake plus transfer.
- They are often a good first mechanism for newer teams or teams short on time.

That said, `simple` does not mean `automatically good`. If the gamepiece or scoring task punishes slow pickup, the simpler mechanism may still be the worse robot choice.

## Maintenance and Reliability

Overall maintenance complexity is usually `low to medium`.

- Fewer moving parts usually means fewer things to fail.
- Check pivots, servo horns, and mounting points often.
- Watch for bent arms, cracked grippers, or loosened fasteners after impacts.
- Make sure students can replace the gripper tips, elastic parts, or worn contact surfaces quickly.

## Good Starting Point

- Start with the simplest geometry that securely captures the actual gamepiece.
- Prototype around the real object first, not just dimensions on paper.
- If the same mechanism can collect and score without a separate transfer, that is often the best passive starting point.
- If your team is deciding between a simple passive design and a complicated active one it may never finish, the passive option can be the smarter first robot.

## Compare It to Other Intake Options

- Compared with an active intake, this style usually gives better direct one-piece control but worse cycle speed.
- Compared with a [Compliant or Flap Wheel Intake](compliant-or-flap-wheel-intake.md), it usually asks more of the driver and less of the mechanism.
- Compared with a [Horizontal Roller Intake](horizontal-roller-intake.md), it usually gives up forgiving floor pickup for simplicity.
- For the broad comparison, go back to the [Active vs Passive Intake Comparison](../intake-quick-reference.md#active-vs-passive-intake-comparison).

## What Still Needs Confirmation

- Confirm that the current season's gamepiece and scoring tasks really reward direct control enough to justify slower pickup.
- Confirm the current season rules, legal parts, and any possession limits.
- Re-check whether a passive intake still leaves enough match time for the number of cycles your strategy needs.

# Mechanical Best Practices

Keep this page short on purpose. The goal is to give students a few strong habits without overwhelming them.

## Best practices

- Start simple
  Choose a mechanism your team can build, repair, and test reliably before chasing a more complex version.
  Learn more: [GM0 Design Tradeoffs](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/design-tradeoffs.html)

- Build for stiffness
  Robots that flex too much are harder to drive, tune, and repair.
  Learn more: [GM0 Hardware Tips and Tricks](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/hardware-components/tips-and-tricks.html)

- Make maintenance easy
  Leave room to reach fasteners, belts, chains, and electronics without tearing the whole robot apart.
  Learn more: [GM0 Fastener Guide](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/hardware-components/fastener-guide.html)

- Align power transmission carefully
  Poor chain, belt, gear, or shaft alignment creates friction and failures.
  Learn more: [GM0 Power Transmission](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/power-transmission/index.html)

- Prototype before committing
  A quick test build can save a lot of time compared to discovering flaws after a full build.
  Learn more: [GM0 Engineering Design Process](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/design-skills/engineering-design-process.html)

- Pick mechanisms that match the game job
  Do not choose a slide, arm, intake, or drivetrain just because another team used it.
  Learn more: [GM0 Common Mechanisms](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/common-mechanisms/index.html)
